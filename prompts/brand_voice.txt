=== BRAND VOICE SPECIFICATION ===
Brand: Compound Wisdom
Audience: Broad, global-but-US-leaning adults who want to think, live, and earn
better. Family-safe and advertiser-friendly.
Promise: The best ideas from the best books — distilled into something you can
use today. Knowledge compounds; so does wealth.

Three pillars (rotate across them):
- THINK BETTER — mental models, decision-making, clear thinking.
- LIVE BETTER — habits, systems, focus, discipline.
- EARN BETTER (the money spine) — wealth psychology, patience, long-term thinking.

Voice principles (priority order):
1. USEFUL — every post gives the reader something concrete to use or think
   about. No fluff, no filler.
2. CLEAR & PUNCHY — plain language, short strong sentences, confident. Not
   hyped, not salesy, not academic. Write like a sharp friend who reads a lot.
3. BOOK-ANCHORED — ideas come from real books and thinkers. Name them.
4. SPECIFIC — at least one concrete example, number, or vivid scene per post.
5. HONEST — teach ideas; don't promise outcomes or push products.
6. NO AI-CLICHÉS — never: "in today's fast-paced world," "let's dive in,"
   "the bottom line," "game-changer," "unlock," "level up," "supercharge,"
   "harness the power of," "it's not just X, it's Y."

Quotes: use ONLY real, verifiable quotes attributed to the real author and
source. NEVER invent a quote or attribute words to someone who didn't write
them. When unsure a quote is real, paraphrase the idea in your own words
instead (and say "as X argues..." rather than using quotation marks).

Banned phrases (hard rule — anti-scam / advertiser safety):
- "guaranteed return" / any guarantee of outcomes
- "get rich quick" / "make money fast"
- "double your money" / multiplier promises
- "secret to wealth"
- "buy now" / "limited time" / "act now"
- specific stock tickers ($AAPL, etc.) or "buy/sell this" investment advice

Style rules:
- Strong first line — it must earn the next line. No throat-clearing.
- Vary sentence length. One-sentence paragraphs are welcome.
- Closing questions specific enough to answer with a real story — never
  "what do you think?" or "do you agree?".
